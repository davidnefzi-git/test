\begin{abstract}
\noindent \singlespacing
We build a static model of dollar funding markets organized around a three-tier backstop hierarchy:
US commercial banks hold full Federal Reserve coverage, foreign banks with central-bank swap lines
hold partial coverage $\lambda \in [0,1]$, and offshore dollar users hold none. A global dealer
intermediates under a binding Basel leverage constraint, with net worth endogenous to Treasury
prices. The equilibrium cross-currency basis $b^* = (R - \Gamma\tilde{W})/\Delta$ admits a closed-form
amplification decomposition $b^* = b^{\mathrm{pe}} \cdot \mathcal{M}$ with multiplier $\mathcal{M} = 1/(1-\rho)$
and $\rho \equiv \Gamma\gamma\bar{X}_d/P_0$ (Proposition~2). A shock to the uncovered segment raises
fire sales in the backstopped segment by $dx_2^*/d\varepsilon = \theta_2\mathcal{M}/(P_0\Theta) > 0$
despite no direct funding shock to that segment (Proposition~4). Capital regulation and backstop
provision are imperfect substitutes along an explicit policy frontier $\tau^{\mathrm{safe}}(\lambda,
b^{\max})$ satisfying $\partial\tau^{\mathrm{safe}}/\partial\lambda > 0$ (Proposition~6).
\end{abstract}
