# Audit and reorganisation, October 2026

What was wrong, what was changed, what was verified, and what remains open.

The library's purpose is to be a **scientific reference** that a reasoning system can
rely on: to recover a canonical model's specification, understand a component's economic
foundations, and determine the conditions under which a mechanism can be reused
elsewhere. This audit judged the files against that purpose, not against their own
presentation documents.

Contents: §1 what was examined and how. §2 the defects found, by severity. §3 what was
changed and why. §4 what was not changed, and why not. §5 validation. §6 what remains
open.

---

## 1. Method

The diagnosis was built from the files, mechanically where possible. Three scripts read
every component, manifest, schema and taxonomy entry and compared declarations against
the equations beside them: identifiers used against identifiers declared, parameters used
against parameters reachable, referenced equations against equations that exist, declared
incompatibilities against their reciprocals. Everything in §2 under **interfaces** came
from those scripts rather than from reading.

The theoretical and provenance axes cannot be checked mechanically and were read.

One thing was deliberately **not** done: no attempt was made to re-verify the equations
against the printed publications. Where a correspondence was already recorded as
unconfirmed, it is still recorded as unconfirmed; §6 lists those cases. Claiming a
verification that was not performed would be the one failure this library cannot afford.

## 2. Defects found

### 2.1 Interfaces — severe

**`calvo-prices` declared the wrong interface, in both directions.** Its equation uses
real marginal cost `mc` and declared neither providing nor requiring it, while declaring
`x`, the output gap, which the equation no longer contains. The equation had been
rewritten from the output-gap form into the marginal-cost form in an earlier change and
the declaration was never updated.

Both halves matter and for different reasons. An **undeclared requirement** defeats the
composition check: the engine verifies that everything a component requires is provided,
so this block could be mounted on a core supplying no marginal cost and the error would
surface only at matrix assembly, as an unresolved symbol. A **stale requirement** asserts
a dependency the equations do not have, which is what a reader would use to decide
whether a block fits a model.

**`compatible_cores` was declared in the schema and enforced nowhere.** It appeared in no
engine code and no test. That would be merely untidy if the field were decorative, and it
is not: for `price-indexation` and `wage-indexation` it is the **only** place recording
that their equations use parameters declared by `core-nk-medium`, since their `depends_on`
names `calvo-prices-kimball`. A field that carries a real dependency and is enforced
nowhere is worse than an absent one, because it reads as a contract.

**One symbol named two unrelated economic objects.** `phi_p` was the fixed-cost share in
`core-nk-medium`, default 1.6, and the Rotemberg price adjustment cost in
`rotemberg-prices`, default 372.79. The assembler refuses to compose two components
declaring the same parameter, so a medium-scale core with Rotemberg pricing — a perfectly
sensible model — failed with "parameter declared by two components", a message that reads
like a bug rather than a name clash. And a reader encountering `phi_p` in a formula had no
way to tell which object it was.

### 2.2 Interfaces — moderate

**An incompatibility was one-sided.** `calvo-wages-kimball` declared itself incompatible
with `calvo-wages`; the reverse declaration was absent. The assembler would still have
refused the composition, for the right reason — both provide the same variable — while the
metadata said nothing, so a reader consulting only `calvo-wages` would have concluded the
two compose.

### 2.3 Provenance and scientific status — severe

**No field distinguished an equation's status with respect to its source.** `source` is
free text, and 16 of the 78 equations carried, *in prose*, a signal that the relation was
a rearrangement, a specialisation, or of uncertain provenance: "rearranged", "at lambda =
0", "at zero adjustment cost", "per the annotation of a third-party Dynare replication",
"Standard". Three equations had no source at all. The remaining 59 looked original and the
field could not say so.

For a library whose purpose is to be relied on, this is the central defect. A reader
cannot be asked to infer from prose whether a Phillips curve is the publication's, an
algebraic rearrangement of it, a deliberate departure from it, or unverified.

**Two derivation pointers led nowhere.** `calvo-wages.real-wage-gap` and
`calvo-wages.mc-sticky-wage` cited "derivation in spec/model.md of ehl-2000". That
document did not exist in this tree. Two of the three equations of the wage block were
therefore `derived` with no derivation anywhere.

### 2.4 Provenance — moderate

**Contradictory verification metadata.** `rotemberg-prices.verification.notes` stated that
the first-order equivalence with Calvo "is not tested here"; its `open_questions` carried
an item prefixed `RESOLVED` saying that the equivalence "is now tested". The two fields
contradicted each other, and a resolved item sat in the field reserved for unresolved
ones. A reader taking `open_questions` as a list of what remains open would have concluded
that the component's central property was unestablished — and taking `notes` at face value
would have concluded the same thing twice.

**A stale open question.** `calvo-prices` listed indexation as "a separate component (not
yet written)". `price-indexation` exists. It happens to depend on
`calvo-prices-kimball` rather than on `calvo-prices`, so the statement was wrong in a
second way as well.

**Seven broken internal file references.** Documents and components pointed at files
that do not exist: a `decisions.md` under `docs/` and two test files whose names belong to
the tree this prototype was extracted from rather than to this one, and a path written
relative to a model folder when the file sits at the root. (Those names are given here
without their directories deliberately: the test described below treats a path-shaped
string in any document as a live reference, so naming a missing file in full would make
this audit fail its own check.)
Three were introduced by this reorganisation itself — a test file I renamed after writing
the pointer to it. Nothing caught any of them, because a prose reference was a documentary
convention rather than a checked one. In a library whose purpose is that a claim can be
traced to where it is established, a pointer to nothing is the defect the purpose exists
to rule out.

### 2.5 Theoretical completeness — severe

**The components documented equations, not foundations.** Each carries its linearised
equations, its parameters with sources, and prose on how the equation composes. None
carried the primitives: no aggregator, no agent's optimisation problem, no first-order
condition, no aggregation step, no linearisation.

That is sufficient to verify a solution and insufficient for the library's purpose.
Having the Euler equation with habit does not allow it to be reintroduced correctly into
an economy whose budget constraint, assets or household structure differ. Having the
Phillips curve does not allow the pricing problem to be reconstructed without the
aggregation technology, the competitive structure and the firm's optimality conditions.
This was the gap the mission identified, and the files confirmed it.

**Three of the four canonical models had no theoretical document at all** — a manifest
naming components and a file of numerical targets. The fourth,
`smets-wouters-2007/spec/model.md`, documented the library's design decisions for that
entry rather than the model.

### 2.6 Model-level metadata — moderate

Three of the four manifests lacked `citation`, `economics`, `shocks` and `provenance`
entirely. `common_variable_map` was absent from three and **wrong** in the fourth:
`nk-gali-2015-ch3` mapped `outputgap`, `inflationq` and `interest` — the Macroeconomic
Model Data Base's spellings, which no family in this library declares — while the nine
names its own family declares went unreported. The family contract was nominal.

Mechanism tags were essentially undeclared: zero tags for three models, one for the
fourth, against a taxonomy of eighteen.

### 2.7 What was already sound

Worth recording, because the audit's job is also to leave things alone.

`taxonomy/variables.yaml` is in good order: 36 variables, every one used by at least one
equation, every one carrying both `timing` and `units`, several with explanatory notes
about the failure a wrong reading produces. The timing convention for the capital stock
and the warning about the real wage gap's lag are the kind of thing this file exists for.

The composition engine does what it claims: the five composition checks, the dependency
and incompatibility handling, the derived-parameter resolution in dependency order and the
generalized Schur solver were left untouched apart from the one check added in §3.

The existing tests were left in place and still pass unchanged. `tests/test_coverage.py`
in particular encodes three rules — an entry with targets must have a manifest, every
shock an entry declares must be fired, the response must be non-degenerate at the
calibration used — each of which came from a real defect.

## 3. What was changed

### 3.1 A scientific status on every equation

Two fields on each equation in `schema/component.schema.json`:

- **`status`**: `original` (attested in the cited publication), `derived` (obtained
  mathematically from it), `adapted` (its assumptions deliberately changed), `standard`
  (a textbook relation or accounting identity not specific to the work cited), or
  `unverified` (source or correspondence not established).
- **`derivation`**: where the steps are written out. **Required** when the status is
  `derived` or `adapted`, and a test checks that the pointer resolves.

All 78 equations were classified: **57 original, 14 derived, 7 standard**. No equation is
`adapted` and none is `unverified`, and both of those deserve a word.

Nothing is `adapted` because no equation in the library departs from its source's
assumptions. The two places where the library differs from a *reference implementation* —
the risk-premium wedge's sign and scale, and the factor on the investment shock in the
capital accumulation equation — are differences between the **article** and that
implementation, and the library follows the article. Those are `original`.

Nothing is `unverified`, and that was a judgement rather than an absence. Six equations
carry equation numbers taken from the annotations of a third-party Dynare replication
rather than from a printed edition. The *relations* are standard and not in doubt; the
*numbering* is second-hand. Marking them `unverified` would tell a reader that Galí's IS
curve may not be Galí's, which is false and is the worse error: understating reliability
misleads as much as overstating it. They are `original`, the caveat stays in `source`, and
all three affected components record it in `verification.open_questions`. §6 lists them.

### 3.2 Theory documents, mutualised by mechanism

Four documents under `docs/theory/`, plus an index, totalling about 1,200 lines:

| document | derives |
|---|---|
| `price-setting.md` | Dixit–Stiglitz and Kimball aggregation, the firm's reset problem, firm-specific against average marginal cost, the price index, the Calvo and indexed curves, Rotemberg and its first-order equivalence |
| `wage-setting.md` | differentiated labour, the wage markup, staggered wage setting, and **in full** the two identities `calvo-wages` relies on |
| `consumption-and-capital.md` | habit preferences, the Euler equation and the marginal rate of substitution, utilisation, adjustment costs, Tobin's q, accumulation, trend growth, and the five closures of the medium-scale core |
| `open-economy.md` | relative prices and the two inflation rates, the real exchange rate, risk sharing, the openness composites, the four regimes |

**Why four documents and not twenty-four.** The derivations are shared. One Calvo
derivation serves five pricing components; one habit derivation serves `habit-consumption`
and two closures of the core. A document per component would have duplicated each
derivation up to five times, and §9.3 of the mission is right that independently
maintained copies diverge. `fisher-equation` needs no derivation document at all, and
does not have one.

**Each document ends with a table headed "what a transplant requires"**, listing which
steps of the derivation fail when the environment changes. That table is the part written
for reuse rather than for reading, and it is what answers the mission's central
requirement: the linearised equations are not universally transposable, and the library
now says where they stop.

**What the documents do not claim.** Three places state that a step is reproduced in
outline only or that a relation is the source's normalisation rather than a result: the
Kimball slope in `price-setting.md` §5, the utilisation-cost parameter in
`consumption-and-capital.md` §3, and the investment shock's scaling in §4.2. Those
boundaries are marked in place rather than in a footnote.

### 3.3 Economic contracts: the `assumptions` field

A structured field on each component listing the economic assumptions it **maintains**, as
opposed to the friction it introduces. Each entry names the assumption, states it, names
the equations in which it is operative, says whether it is the cited work's or the
library's, and — where the consequences are known — what replacing it would require.

**52 assumptions across the 24 components**, seven of them marked `library` rather than
`canonical`.

This is the mission's §5.1 point made structural. A composition check establishes that the
equation count closes and that no variable is provided twice. It cannot establish that two
blocks rest on the same view of the economy: two components can agree on every variable
name and still disagree about whether markets are complete, how expectations are formed,
or what an asset return is.

The field's most useful entries are the ones that refuse a false simplicity.
`core-soe-gm.complete-international-markets` does not say "incompatible with incomplete
markets"; it says that replacing it requires four coupled changes and names them.
`habit-consumption.external-habit` says that internal habit gives the Euler equation a
second lead and is not a reparameterisation. A compatibility flag would have implied that
either swap was free.

### 3.4 `compatible_cores` enforced

The assembler now refuses a composition whose core is absent from a component's
`compatible_cores`, when that list is non-empty, with a message saying which. A test
additionally checks that every parameter a component's equations use is declared by some
component reachable through `depends_on` or `compatible_cores`. The field has become the
contract it read as.

### 3.5 A registry of shared parameter symbols

`taxonomy/parameters.yaml`: the nine symbols that more than one component declares, each
with the meaning they share and, where the reading differs between families, a note.

The alternative considered and rejected was a test comparing the components'
free-text descriptions. It does not work: "Discount factor" and "Quarterly discount
factor" differ as strings and not as objects, so the test produced six false positives
and would have been silenced. Whether two declarations mean the same thing is a question
for a declaration, not a string comparison.

`alpha` is the entry worth reading. It is the same exponent in both cores and a different
economic object: a decreasing-returns parameter where there is no capital, a capital share
where there is — and, in the medium-scale case, not a capital *income* share, because the
fixed cost drives a wedge between the two. The registry says so rather than pretending the
two readings are one.

### 3.6 The `phi_p` collision resolved

The Rotemberg parameter is now `phi_rotemberg`. **This is an incompatible change** and
§9.5 of the mission requires it to be documented:

- **Motive**: one symbol named two unrelated objects, two orders of magnitude apart.
- **Files**: `components/rotemberg-prices/component.yaml`,
  `models/nk-gali-2015-ch3/golden/targets.yaml`, `tests/test_levels.py`.
- **Behaviour change**: none. The parameter's value, role and equation are unchanged;
  only its name differs. An external caller passing `{"phi_p": ...}` to a Rotemberg
  composition must now pass `phi_rotemberg`.
- **Blast radius**: `rotemberg-prices` is a variant component that no entry composes. It
  is exercised by one nesting target and one equivalence test, both updated.
- **Evidence that the canonical models are unaffected**: §5.

### 3.7 Metadata harmonised, documents written

The four manifests were completed: `citation`, `economics` with mechanism tags, `shocks`
with their normalisations and sign conventions, `provenance`. `nk-gali-2015-ch3`'s
`common_variable_map` was rewritten in its family's vocabulary and three unreported
variables were explained rather than left absent.

Each of the four models now has a `spec/model.md` giving the environment, the agents'
problems, the complete equation system, the steady state, the parameters, the theoretical
properties, and — a section each has — what the library's representation simplifies.
About 600 lines in total.

The contradictory and stale verification notes were harmonised, with the contradiction
recorded in `notes` rather than deleted, because the risk it created is specific and worth
remembering.

### 3.8 Validation that can be mechanised

`tests/test_knowledge_base.py`, 304 tests. Each corresponds to a defect found in §2 rather
than to a defect imagined; the docstrings say which. They check declared variables against
used variables in both directions, unresolved identifiers, parameter reachability,
referential integrity of `replaces` / `depends_on` / `incompatible_with` /
`compatible_cores`, reciprocity of incompatibility, registration of shared symbols,
presence of a status on every equation, resolution of every derivation pointer, attachment
of every assumption to an equation that exists, absence of resolved items in
`open_questions`, resolution of every internal file reference in prose and in metadata,
and at the model level: family vocabulary, mechanism tags, and the presence of a theory
document.

**What these tests deliberately do not do is certify economics.** No test establishes that
a Phillips curve is Galí's, that two blocks are theoretically compatible, or that a
derivation is correct. Those require mathematics and reading, and the library's answers to
them are the `status` field, the theory documents and the open questions — not a green
tick. The tests certify what is mechanically verifiable and make the theoretical questions
visible.

## 4. What was not changed, and why

**Complete markets was not extracted from `core-soe-gm` into a component.** The mission
invited the question. Against it: the equation is already isolated, named and documented
as a maintained assumption that an incomplete-markets block would replace, and the
`assumptions` field now states the four coupled changes such a replacement requires.
Extracting it would leave the core not closed on its own, would change every existing
composition of that family, and would deliver a documentation benefit the field already
delivers. The mission's own condition — extract only if it genuinely improves coherence
and reusability — is not met.

**No equation, parameter, normalisation or equilibrium condition was modified.** §5 is the
evidence.

**No new artefact type, no document per component, no external index, no estimation
machinery.** The library has four canonical models and two dozen components; the structure
added is two schema field groups, one taxonomy file, five theory documents, four model
specifications and one test file.

**The `unverified` status is unused**, by the judgement recorded in §3.1. It remains in
the schema because the case it names is real and will occur.

**`reviewed_by` is `null` on all 24 components**, as it was. It means no human has
reviewed them. Filling it would be false.

## 5. Validation

### 5.1 Non-regression: the canonical models are numerically identical

Every variable's impulse response to every shock, twelve periods, computed in a worktree
at the branch point and in the reorganised tree, and compared:

| model | size | worst difference |
|---|---|---|
| `nk-gali-2015-ch3` | 9 eq / 9 var | **0** |
| `ehl-2000` | 11 eq / 11 var | **0** |
| `gali-monacelli-2005` | 16 eq / 16 var | **0** |
| `smets-wouters-2007` | 37 eq / 37 var | **0** |

Not "small": exactly zero, in every series. No parameter was renamed or revalued in any of
the four compositions — the `phi_rotemberg` rename touches a variant no entry composes.
The reorganisation is documentary in effect as well as in intent.

### 5.2 The test suite

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| tests passing | 167 | **471** |
| failing | 0 | 0 |
| runtime | 36 s | 35 s |

The 304 additions are the knowledge-base validations. Every pre-existing test passes
unchanged; none was weakened or deleted.

### 5.3 What the tests established, and what they did not

**Established mechanically**: that no equation uses an undeclared variable or an
unresolved identifier; that no declared requirement is unused; that every parameter used
is reachable; that every reference to a component or equation resolves; that every
incompatibility is reciprocal; that every shared symbol is registered; that every equation
declares a status; that every derivation pointer resolves to a declared anchor; that every
assumption names equations that exist; that no resolved item sits in `open_questions`;
that each model declares its family's vocabulary and valid mechanism tags and carries a
theory document.

**Confirmed by reading and derivation, not by a test**: the two identities of the wage
block, derived in full in `docs/theory/wage-setting.md` §4 and §5, which previously
pointed at a document that did not exist. The first-order equivalence between Rotemberg
and Calvo, derived in `price-setting.md` §6 and separately tested numerically.

**Not established, and not claimed**: that the equations correspond to the printed
publications. No publication was consulted during this work. Where a correspondence was
recorded as unconfirmed it is still recorded as unconfirmed.

## 6. What remains open

### 6.1 Requiring consultation of the publications

**Six equation numbers are second-hand** — three in `core-nk-classical` (`euler`,
`natural-rate`, `natural-output`), two in `calvo-wages` (`wage-pc`, `real-wage-gap`), one
in `calvo-prices` (`nkpc`). They come from a third-party Dynare replication's annotations.
The relations are not in doubt; the numbering is. Each component records it.

**The Kimball slope's derivation** is reproduced in outline only. Establishing the step
from the implicit aggregator to the factor $(\phi_p-1)\varepsilon_p+1$ requires the
second-order properties of the aggregator at the symmetric point, given in Smets and
Wouters's appendix and in Kimball (1995).

**Galí and Monacelli's equation numbers** were read from the article and cross-read
against a third party's annotations, which agree. That is better than second-hand and
short of confirmed.

### 6.2 Theoretical, and out of this mission's scope

**Second order**, and everything needing it: every welfare statement in the library's four
articles — Galí–Monacelli's regime ranking, Erceg–Henderson–Levin's welfare losses,
Smets–Wouters's welfare analysis. The equations are supplied log-linearised and no entry
claims a welfare result.

**Unconditional moments**, which Smets–Wouters's variance decomposition and Hansen's
reported moments both need. A moments tier also needs a detrending convention for the
open-economy family, whose three inflation-based regimes leave a unit root; that
convention belongs in `taxonomy/families.yaml`.

**Estimation.** Priors and the data contract are declared and validated; nothing samples
from them. Explicitly outside this mission.

### 6.3 Gaps in coverage the audit exposed

**No indexation block in Galí's parameterisation.** `price-indexation` was written for the
medium-scale family. Whether to add one or to unify the two pricing blocks is an open
question, and `docs/theory/price-setting.md` derives both from the same primitives, which
is what makes unification thinkable.

**No divisible-labour variant for `core-rbc-hansen`**, which is the comparison Hansen's
article is about. The library carries the indivisible case and says so.

**`credit_spread` is a declared common variable of the `monetary-dsge` family that no
entry reports**, because no financial friction exists in the library. The family declares
an aspiration; three of its entries cannot meet it.

**Nothing is human-reviewed.** `reviewed_by` is null throughout. Every verification claim
in this library rests on its tests, its closed forms and its cross-checks against
independent implementations — not on a second pair of eyes.
