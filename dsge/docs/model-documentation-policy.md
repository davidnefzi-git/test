# Model documentation policy

The library separates the **economic content of a canonical model** from the software
used to verify that content. Model documentation must follow the methodology of the
canonical source rather than impose one uniform empirical template on every entry.

## 1. Resources required for every model

Every canonical model entry contains:

- `manifest.yaml`: model identity, composition, shocks, economic scope and provenance;
- `spec/model.md`: the theoretical environment, equilibrium system, steady state,
  parameters, model-specific simplifications and theoretical properties;
- `golden/targets.yaml`: enduring analytical or numerical verification targets.

Theoretical derivations shared by several entries belong in `docs/theory/` and are
referenced from components rather than duplicated in model specifications.

## 2. Theoretical or calibrated models

If the canonical source is theoretical, analytically solved or calibrated without an
estimation procedure that is part of the model's contribution, do **not** add an
estimation section merely for symmetry with estimated models.

Document instead:

- primitives and maintained assumptions;
- equilibrium conditions;
- steady state and approximation;
- calibrated parameters and their sources;
- analytical propositions, impulse responses or comparative-statics results that define
  the canonical exercise.

Examples include the Galí chapter-3 and chapter-6 benchmarks and the
Galí–Monacelli small-open-economy model as represented here.

## 3. Estimated models

If the published model is defined in part by an empirical estimation procedure, that
procedure is part of the canonical information and must be documented.

The model entry should then add:

- `estimation.yaml`: structured source facts required to understand or reproduce the
  published estimation;
- `spec/estimation.md`: the statistical and economic interpretation of those facts.

For a Bayesian DSGE, the estimation reference should record, when supplied by the
primary source:

- observables and measurement equations;
- data frequency, sample and transformations;
- estimated, calibrated and derived parameters;
- prior distributions and their parameterization;
- structural shock distributions and covariance restrictions;
- likelihood and state-space interpretation;
- posterior-mode optimization and posterior-sampling procedure;
- MCMC settings and convergence diagnostics;
- posterior modes, means and uncertainty intervals;
- marginal-likelihood or model-comparison criteria;
- posterior impulse responses, variance decompositions, historical decompositions or
  other reported empirical objects.

For a model estimated by another method, document the corresponding method rather than
forcing it into a Bayesian template.

## 4. What the estimation resources are not

An estimation reference is **not** an estimator implementation. Published posterior
numbers or point estimates are source data, not outputs generated by this repository.

Do not add a Kalman filter, MCMC sampler, optimizer or data-download workflow merely
because the canonical article uses one. Those tools may be developed separately for a
replication exercise.

Do not record execution logs, local run times or temporary diagnostics in the scientific
model files.

## 5. Calibration versus estimation

A calibrated parameter belongs in the component or model metadata with its source.
An estimated parameter additionally belongs in the model's estimation reference when
the estimation procedure is part of the canonical source.

Do not describe a calibrated model as estimated, and do not reduce an estimated model to
a list of posterior means. In an estimated model, the observable mapping, priors,
likelihood and posterior uncertainty are part of the canonical specification.

## 6. Source hierarchy

Use the final published article or book chapter as the controlling source whenever it is
available. Working papers and public replication materials may be used to clarify
notation or implementation details, but divergences must be identified explicitly and
must not silently replace the published specification.
